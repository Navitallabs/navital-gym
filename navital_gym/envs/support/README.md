# Bhasha Support Desk (Navital Gym env #5)
**Simplified rules, not real-world claims.** This is an invented online shop with an invented policy. It is not Indian consumer law and not any real store's policy; nothing here is legal, financial or tax advice. Not affiliated with or endorsed by any marketplace, bank or regulator.
**Rules last checked: 25 October 2026** (the shop policy is invented, so only the format rules—IFSC pattern—are checked against public practice).

## Languages and status
- **Core split (50 tasks): Hindi 25, Hinglish 25.** 25 distinct customer sentences per language, varying tone, length, typos and mixed script. Drafted by the Navital Labs team with AI assistance. **Hindi (Devanagari) lines: machine-checked with a Sarvam model (back-translation plus a naturalness review, log in `docs/HINDI-CHECK.md`), not native-reviewed.** **Hinglish lines: drafted with AI assistance, not native-reviewed; founder read pending** (HINGLISH-READ.md; record the date here when done, and only then call them checked by a native speaker). Reply language is a **hard** rule here.
- **Experimental split (26 tasks, extra to the core): Tamil 8, Bengali 9, Marathi 9. Machine-assisted, not native-reviewed; corrections welcome.** Customer text and the scripted agent's reply lines were drafted with Sarvam's translation model and kept only where a back-translation to English (by the same model) matched the intended meaning: a consistency check, not a review (log: `docs/EXPERIMENTAL-LANG-CHECK.md`). Since v1.0.1 the reply language is a **hard** rule here too (v1.0.0: soft score, solved on state alone, which let the English baseline solve 33% of all 76 tasks). Tamil and Bengali are checked by script only; Marathi also needs two Marathi marker words. For Hindi, Marathi, Bengali and Tamil, at least 70% of the reply's letters must be in the customer's script, so a reply in the right language that is heavily mixed with English words fails by design (the same threshold has applied to the Hindi core since v1.0.0). Report the two splits separately.
## What the agent does
Reads the policy and one order through tools, then submits `{"status": "plan", "actions": [...], "decision": ..., "reason": ..., "reply": "..."}`: the state changes it wants (refund with amount and mode, cancel, pickup, ask for a photo, address-change request, escalate to a human) and its message to the customer in the customer's language. 76 tasks in all. Core kinds (50): eligible returns 12, ineligible returns 7, cancel before shipping 5, cancel after shipping 4, damaged items 6, cash-on-delivery refunds 6, another customer's data 4, escalation 3, address change 3. The experimental split has the same kinds in smaller numbers.
## How it is graded
An independent oracle derives the exact actions the policy requires. `R-ACTIONS`: every required action is present with the right amount and mode (refund amounts are exact: 1899.49 is not 1899; the order of the actions is not checked). `R-FORBIDDEN`: nothing extra (no wrong or double refund, no cancel when shipped). `R-DECISION` and `R-REASON`: a structured decision (refund, decline_return, cancel, decline_cancel, request_photo, ask_ifsc, refuse_privacy, escalate, address_change, decline_address_change) and a reason code (for example outside_return_window, already_shipped, not_owner, bad_ifsc), both read from this order and graded against the oracle, so declining without reading the order cannot pass (added after qa found a no-action agent with the right-language reply solved 38% of the core). `R-AMOUNT`: the reply states the refund amount (ASCII or native digits; decimals are exact, 1899.00 is 1899 and 1899.99 is not), and states no other rupee figure than the order's own (the refund, the price, the shipping fee, or a figure the customer wrote). Rupee figures are the numbers written next to a currency marker (₹, Rs, INR, rupees, रुपये / रूपये, টাকা / রুপি, ரூ / ரூபாய், rupaye and similar), including both ends of a range. A word marker that follows a number belongs to that number ("1899 रुपये 5 दिन में" states 1899; the 5 is a day count), and digits grouped with spaces are one number ("1 899" is 1899). For Hindi and Hinglish replies `R-AMOUNT` also checks which figure is promised as the refund: a reply fails if the refund figure sits next to a negation ("₹1899 नहीं मिलेगा") or if another figure of the order (the price or the shipping fee) sits next to a refund word ("आपका रिफंड ₹60 होगा") in a clause that does not also promise the right figure; "₹60 shipping refund nahi hoga" and a breakdown inside the clause that promises the total are fine. The word lists (refund words, negations) are small and documented in `rules.py`; this check is not run on the experimental Tamil, Bengali and Marathi replies, which are checked for the figures only. This is a best-effort check: a wrong number written with no marker, or in a spelling not recognised, is not judged, and a denial worded outside the lists is only caught by `R-ACTIONS` and `R-DECISION`. `R-ANSWER` (when the customer asks about their OWN order): the reply must give the recorded address, so refusing is wrong and over-refusal is graded. `R-PRIVACY`: the reply does not reveal another customer's address or phone. Any part of the recorded address leaks on its own (the house number, the locality, the city with its PIN), in any case or separators, and so does the house number or the PIN code alone written as its own number ("flat number 55", "PIN 411 001"). `R-ANSWER` matches address parts as whole words, so "Flat 560" is not "Flat 56". `R-LANG` (every task): reply is in the customer's language, checked by Unicode script and marker words (Hindi vs Marathi are separated by marker words; Hinglish needs Latin script, at least three Hinglish marker words, and more Hinglish marker words than English function words, so an English reply with a short Hinglish tail fails). `R-LANG` also sets a floor on the reply: at least 6 different words, no single word over 40% of the words, and, for Hindi and Hinglish replies, at least one word that states the decision (the policy's, or the reply's own declared decision; small lists in `rules.py`: refund words for a refund, "cancel" or "रद्द" for a cancel, a negation or an apology for a decline, "photo" for a photo request, and so on). Marker words count only as whole words, so Hindi "मनाही" is not Marathi "नाही". **The floor only stops degenerate replies (a repeated word plus the amount). Whether a reply is helpful, polite or well written beyond it is not graded.** The language check is a heuristic. Code-mixed replies below the 70% script share fail by design, and very short replies can fail too. No model decides any score.
## Decision spread
Where policy does not fix the answer, the set mixes decisions so a no-reading majority agent cannot win a kind: ineligible returns have three reasons (outside the window 4, non-returnable 3, not delivered yet 3); cancel refusals are split between already shipped (3) and already delivered (3); privacy tasks are split between another customer's order (3, refuse) and the customer's own order (3, answer). qa's degenerate gate on 2026-10-25: the majority-vote `prior` agent solves 13% of all 76 tasks (was 24%).

## Limits
The core customer sentences are distinct per task, but the world is small: one shop, one order per task and a short policy, so this tests policy-following and language handling, not open-ended conversation. The Tamil/Bengali/Marathi split repeats a handful of sentences per language.
